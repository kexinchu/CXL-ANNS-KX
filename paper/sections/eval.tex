\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

We answer five questions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{Q1}] Does naive memory-semantic access expose NAND stalls during graph search?
  \item[\textbf{Q2}] At matched recall, how do the original- and optimized-layout Demand baselines, PipeANN, and \sys{} compare in mean latency and throughput?
  \item[\textbf{Q3}] Do the Wise Prefetcher and continuous batching each contribute to the result?
  \item[\textbf{Q4}] Does the benefit survive cold starts and changes in CXL-side cache capacity?
  \item[\textbf{Q5}] Where does \sys{} still lose, and what resources does it consume?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:eval-setup}

We follow established ANNS evaluation practice~\cite{annbench,bigannchallenge}:
every performance comparison reports recall, and any speedup claim uses the
same recall operating point. Table~\ref{tab:setup} summarizes the platform.

\begin{table}[t]
  \caption{Evaluation platform and fixed experiment protocol.}
  \label{tab:setup}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.30\columnwidth}p{0.63\columnwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    Item & Configuration \\
    \midrule
    Host & Intel Xeon 6710E, 64 cores, 32\,GiB DRAM; one NUMA node \\
    Host-resident state & graph, PQ-64 codes, and 128\,MiB scoring window per search thread \\
    CXL-side cache & 4\,GiB \texttt{vmem\_sw} page cache unless varied explicitly \\
    Flash backing & two 1.92\,TB Dell CD8P NVMe SSDs, 2\,MiB striping, exposed through \texttt{/dev/vmem0} \\
    Datasets & T2I-10M (200-d, MIPS), YFCC-10M (192-d, L2), LAION-10M (512-d, MIPS) \\
    Index/search & 10M vectors, Vamana $R{=}32$, PQ-64 navigation, committed full-precision reranking \\
    Protocol & 10,000 fixed queries; five repetitions per mark; cold runs begin at \texttt{cache\_used}${}=0$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The primary configuration uses eight search threads. We sweep $L$ to expose
the recall--performance frontier rather than selecting whichever point makes a
system look best. Error bars are 95\% bootstrap intervals over five independent
repetitions. Every internal run uses the same 4\,GiB CXL-side cache and records
host-window residency, CXL-side cache activity, NAND traffic, and critical-path
wait. A run without a declared cold/warm state or five accepted repetitions is
excluded.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Both Demand baselines perform the same PQ-guided search, disable prefetching
and continuous scheduling, and materialize missing full-precision pages
synchronously. \textbf{Demand (original layout)} additionally removes the
extent placement, while \textbf{Demand (optimized layout)} retains it.
\textbf{PipeANN} is the same-machine block-I/O comparison with its own
DiskANN/PipeANN index and PQ navigation.
\textbf{Wise-only} retains the Wise Prefetcher but disables cross-query
continuous scheduling. We do not execute or plot an Oracle system.

\subsection{Q1: Naive CXL-SSD fails}
\label{sec:eval-mot}

Figures~\ref{fig:mot-cliff}--\ref{fig:mot-bubbles} establish the failure mode
used by the rest of the evaluation: a graph walk that synchronously crosses
the memory--Flash boundary repeatedly exposes NAND latency and fails to build
device concurrency. Q2 therefore measures complete search, while Q3 and Q4
separate the decisions that remove this wait from the scoring path.

\subsection{Q2: End-to-end iso-recall}
\label{sec:eval-main}

Figure~\ref{fig:eval-frontiers} reports both required
views of the end-to-end frontier. Both Demand variants and \sys{} have
identical recall at each $L$ because they preserve the same navigation and
candidate set; they differ only in placement, materialization, and scheduling.
PipeANN is plotted at its measured operating points instead of being
interpolated into an artificial match.

At these matched-recall points, throughput improves over Demand (optimized
layout) by
1.83$\times$ on LAION, 2.34$\times$ on T2I, and 2.45$\times$ on YFCC. The
optimized layout alone improves Demand throughput over its original layout by
3.5\%, 8.1\%, and 5.6\%, respectively; the remaining gain therefore cannot be
attributed to placement. The
figures also expose an important boundary: PipeANN remains faster at
several operating points, particularly on LAION-10M. We treat it as a block
I/O performance reference, not as evidence for the deployment property of the
memory interface.

\subsection{Q3: Each module is necessary}
\label{sec:eval-ablate}

Figure~\ref{fig:wise-ablation} isolates the Wise Prefetcher at one thread so
cross-query scheduling cannot explain its gain. Issuing after candidate
commitment removes most blocking time relative to serialized materialization;
fetching only missing committed pages bounds the host-window requests; and
forming extents reduces physical NAND commands without changing the candidate
IDs or recall. Figure~\ref{fig:prefetch-io-efficiency} separately quantifies
the layout and physical-I/O effects with matched-concurrency controls.

Figure~\ref{fig:batching-ablation} then restores concurrency. It compares the
same Wise Prefetcher with and without continuous batching from $T{=}1$ through
$T{=}16$ on every dataset. The dotted line and filled points identify the
primary $T{=}8$ configuration. At $T{=}8$, full \sys{} reaches 600.8 QPS on
T2I, 3016.2 QPS on YFCC, and 421.2 QPS on LAION. Wise-only reaches 559.2,
2933.2, and 406.0 QPS, respectively. Thus the batching contribution is
measurable but smaller than the prefetcher's removal of synchronous NAND wait;
Figure~\ref{fig:load-tail-latency} tests the separate service-level question
under open-loop arrivals.

Co-use placement reduces host-window requests by 5.2\% and raises useful
vector-slot occupancy from 61.46\% to 64.16\% at the same T=8 concurrency.
Across the separately controlled T=1 prefetcher stages, post-commit batching
reduces physical bytes and read amplification by 2.5\% relative to serialized
materialization; extent formation then reduces NAND commands by a further
7.0\% while increasing the mean transfer from 4.05 to 4.35\,KiB. End to end,
the selected path uses 8.3\% fewer physical NAND bytes and 15.7\% fewer NAND
commands than the original-layout control.

On T2I, \sys{} sustains 368.9 of 369.2 offered QPS at the 1.25$\times$
point with 77.7\,ms P99, whereas PipeANN saturates at 295.0 QPS and its P99
rises to 6.75\,s. The other datasets expose the boundary rather than a universal
win: \sys{} tracks the reference rate on YFCC (2930.5 of 2933.2 QPS) and nearly
tracks it on LAION (395.5 of 406.0 QPS), but both saturate at 1.25$\times$ and
PipeANN retains substantially lower tail latency. Continuous batching reduces
P99 versus Wise-only at the reference rate on T2I and YFCC; LAION's larger
payload reaches its I/O/queueing limit earlier.

\subsection{Q4: Cold hide and cache sensitivity}
\label{sec:eval-scale}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.60\textwidth]{figs/hide-robustness.pdf}
  \caption{Host-side hiding and robustness. (a) Host data-dependency stall,
    stacked into waits for required pages and I/O-admission backpressure.
    (b) End-to-end throughput from the same matched Demand--\sys{} runs. (c) A
    true-cold run and its immediate warm partner. (d) T2I-10M sensitivity to
    the CXL-side cache, normalized to the fixed 4\,GiB configuration. Every
    mark summarizes five runs.}
  \Description{Four panels compare host data-dependency stall, throughput,
    cold and warm tail latency, and cache-capacity sensitivity.}
  \label{fig:hide-robustness}
  \label{fig:eval-scale}
\end{figure*}

Figure~\ref{fig:hide-robustness}(a) measures wall-clock intervals in which a
host worker cannot advance because the next required vector data are
unavailable and no other query is runnable; unlike device-fill lifetime, this
does not count I/O overlapped with useful work. Demand stalls for 21.30, 3.91,
and 20.62\,ms/query on T2I, YFCC, and LAION, whereas \sys{} reduces these
values to 2.70, 0.010, and 4.01\,ms/query---an 80.6--99.7\% reduction. The
remaining \sys{} stall is dominated by coverage waits; admission backpressure
is at most 0.12\,ms/query. The score-source audit confirms zero score-triggered
Flash fills and 100\% pre-materialized scoring, so this is an overlap result,
not a change in the scored candidate set. Panel (b) shows the corresponding
1.88--2.39$\times$ end-to-end throughput gain at identical recall. Cold and
immediate warm results remain close, so the result is not created by an
undeclared warmup. Increasing the T2I CXL-side cache from 1 to 8\,GiB raises
throughput from 466.1 to 666.1 QPS, while the fixed 4\,GiB point delivers
595.5 QPS.

\subsection{Q5: Losses, overhead, and fairness}
\label{sec:eval-limit}

\sys{} does not dominate a tuned block-I/O engine at every recall point.
PipeANN is faster where its compact traversal and block interface are a better
fit, while \sys{} pays for the host graph, PQ codes, per-thread scoring
windows, CXL-side cache management, and memory-semantic software path. The
benefit also narrows when the working set is already hot. These costs delimit
the claim: \sys{} shows how to hide Flash fills behind graph-ANNS work in the
three-tier CXL organization; it does not claim that memory semantics erase all
software overhead or make NAND equivalent to DRAM.

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
Across three 10M-vector datasets, the Wise Prefetcher removes synchronous NAND
wait from committed scoring, while continuous batching supplies a smaller,
separately measured scaling benefit through the primary eight-thread serving
configuration.
