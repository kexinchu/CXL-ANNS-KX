\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Graph-based approximate nearest neighbor search (ANNS) is a production serving primitive: a query walks a proximity graph and scores neighbors until a beam budget is spent.
At billions of vectors the full-precision embeddings no longer fit in host DRAM, even when the more compact graph and PQ metadata do, so the vector payload must live behind another tier.
CXL makes that tier addressable with \texttt{load}/\texttt{store}.
When the whole corpus fits in CXL-DRAM, prior systems already offer a usable service---CXL-ANNS and Cosmos~\cite{cxlanns,cosmos} never stall on flash.
This paper is about the next device.
Flash-backed CXL memory exposes Flash capacity through a bounded CXL-DRAM page cache on a CXL.mem-style address space~\cite{skybyte,fromblocktobyte,cmmh,cxl31}.
A query host may keep a still smaller software cache, but a miss in the CXL-DRAM cache costs tens of microseconds because it fills from Flash.
To our knowledge, \sys{} is the first \emph{graph-ANNS serving runtime} that operates directly over an SSD-backed CXL address space and enforces \emph{score hide}.
The host computes from its small cache or from a CXL-DRAM-cache hit; no scoring access may trigger or wait for a Flash fill.
We do not claim the first CXL-DRAM ANNS, the first CXL-SSD attached to a vector database, or a product CXL.mem SSD.

Two published lines each solve one side of the capacity--latency tension and tax the other (Figure~\ref{fig:intro-position}).
DiskANN~\cite{diskann,freshdiskann} and pipelined descendants such as PipeANN~\cite{pipeann} are excellent \emph{block-SSD} ANNS: PQ navigation, sector layout, and asynchronous I/O hide NAND behind a per-host cache.
Their serving unit remains CPU $+$ DRAM working set $+$ a local SSD index.
Scaling the service across hosts commonly replicates or shards the flash-resident index.
They hide \emph{block I/O}, not a memory-semantic hierarchy with two managed cache levels.
CXL-ANNS and Cosmos put the graph and embeddings in a CXL-DRAM pool.
Search never sees flash, but the cold tail $D-H$ is priced at DRAM~\cite{bauhaus,secondtier}.
We build a third service because neither line is the right contract for flash-backed CXL memory: full-precision vectors should stay Flash-priced, compact search information should guide movement from host DRAM, and the runtime must hide Flash fills from the scoring instruction.
Multi-host one-copy under Shared FAM is a deployment path we do not evaluate (\S\ref{sec:background}).

Making that service real is hard because the device is not slightly slow DRAM and not a DiskANN SSD.
Three hardware--search interactions break conventional designs~(\S\ref{sec:motivation}).
\emph{(C1)~The miss cliff.}
A resident CXL-side-cache access avoids physical I/O; on our measured
memory-semantic path, a Flash fill takes $81$--$83\,\mu$s, or
$11$--$20\times$ the latency of a resident VMEM access and more than
$800\times$ a host-window access.
Graph walk multiplies the tax by hops $\times$ degree, so treating the composed hierarchy as uniform mmap'd memory makes search miss-bound.
\emph{(C2)~Prefetch must be wise.}
Neighbor IDs are not sequential.
Blind 16\,KiB admission reads $4.36\times$ more NAND than selective
4\,KiB admission, while Top-8 two-hop speculation reads $13.3\times$ more
than demand materialization on the same committed candidate trace.
Wrong pages cost more than a missed page.
\emph{(C3)~One query cannot fill the device.}
Best-first search cannot issue hop $i{+}1$'s vector pages until hop $i$ scores.
A single query exposes effective QD 0.81--1.49; even 16 concurrent queries
reach only QD 3.23--5.21, far below the measured device knee of 128.
Continuous issue across queries is what holds depth; it does not, by itself, make one walk as fast as an in-window oracle.

\sys{} is a runtime for those three constraints, not a new graph index.
A placement premise comes first: host DRAM holds graph adjacency and compact PQ codes, while full-precision vectors remain in the Flash-backed address space behind a bounded CXL-DRAM page cache.
The first mechanism is a \emph{wise prefetcher}.
After PQ navigation commits the rerank candidates, it maps only their missing full-precision vectors to unique pages and issues those pages before exact scoring.
An offline co-use-aware layout, online page deduplication, and density-bounded coalescing increase the fraction of each transferred page that reranking actually consumes; the prefetcher neither walks a speculative 2-hop neighborhood nor scores unused siblings to inflate utilization.
The second mechanism is \emph{continuous batching}.
Fills are issued at fetch granularity across in-flight queries so that when one walk is stuck on pointer-chasing, another query keeps the device busy.
Prefetch and batching may trigger Flash fills; the scoring thread may access the host cache or CXL-DRAM, but may not trigger or wait for Flash.
We call that split \emph{score hide}.
The stronger contract---\emph{query hide}, no wait for a fill on the timed path---is the oracle, not a result we claim.
Our evaluation therefore reports host-cache hits, CXL-DRAM hits, score-triggered Flash fills, and critical-path Flash wait before reporting iso-recall throughput.
The separate full-corpus CXL-DRAM Oracle removes Flash from the timed path; it is not a second active copy in the normal 4\,GiB-cache configuration.

This paper makes four contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A third serving point, scoped.}
    We place graph ANNS on a three-tier host-cache/CXL-DRAM-cache/Flash hierarchy and separate the \emph{opportunity} (Flash-priced $D$) from the \emph{system} (hide Flash fills from scoring).
    We do not claim the first CXL-DRAM ANNS or an evaluated Shared-FAM deployment~(\S\ref{sec:background}).
  \item \textbf{Why naive CXL-SSD ANNS fails.}
    We measure the miss cliff, unwise page/prefetch admission, and single-query queue collapse, and we turn those negatives into design constraints~(\S\ref{sec:motivation}).
  \item \textbf{A wise prefetcher plus continuous batching.}
    The prefetcher waits for candidate commitment, fetches only missing rerank-vector pages, and shapes them for high useful-page occupancy; batching holds device depth across walks.
    Score hide requires zero score-triggered Flash fills, while the full-corpus CXL-DRAM Oracle remains a separate upper bound~(\S\ref{sec:design}, \S\ref{sec:impl}).
  \item \textbf{An iso-recall evaluation of hide, not just QPS.}
    We report the three-level hit/fill breakdown and critical-path Flash wait before iso-recall latency and throughput.
    Same-machine PipeANN is a named block-I/O comparison, not a hidden bar~(\S\ref{sec:eval}).
\end{enumerate}
